## 1. The flow, per job

1. **Roro takes the call**, gives the price on the phone, sends Afrim.
2. **Afrim does the work.** He does NO paperwork, no invoice, no numbers, no maths. One exception, section 2.
3. **Roro sends Steph ONE WhatsApp message** with the job details, using the template in section 3. He sends it the same day, ideally from the van door or straight after the call.
4. **Steph makes the invoice** in the Word file: Dutch customer, Dutch file; French customer, French file (`assets/prepared/invoice/README.md`). She fills only what Roro sent her, in the order he sent it, because the template follows the invoice's own field order.
5. **She numbers it in order, continuing her OWN existing series** (Fady, 2026-08-30). She keeps the numbering she already uses and carries on from her last one. No gaps, no reuse, no skipping. The number is decided by the ORDER the invoices are made, never by the job date. The new Word file preloads no number and its label reads "N° de facture"; the old paper said "N° DE DEVIS" on a page titled FACTURE, and that label was the only thing wrong with it.
6. **She saves it as PDF and sends it**, by e-mail or WhatsApp, from info@prodebouchage24.be. Paper copy only when the customer wants paper.
7. **The money.** Roro tells her in the same message how it was paid. Cash or Bancontact on the spot: she writes the paid line on the invoice ("Facture acquittée, paiement reçu en espèces" for cash, the habit already used on the old invoices). Not yet paid: the invoice goes out with the IBAN and "payable à réception", and she keeps it on a short "waiting for money" list. Cash is capped at 3.000 euro per job by law.
8. **Every invoice is kept 10 years**, PDF plus the Word file.

### The two rules that never bend here

- **One invoice for EVERY job.** Small job, night job, cash job, family price: all of them. In this trade there is no threshold and no cash exemption (research/26 section 1).
- **The deadline is the 15th of the month AFTER the month of the work.** A job on 3 September must be invoiced by 15 October at the latest. That is the law's outer limit, and it is a trap, because the oldest job of the month is the one that gets forgotten. **The working rule is: same week, always.** Sunday evening, Steph clears everything from that week. Nothing older than seven days is ever waiting.

## 2. The work order, the one thing Afrim has to carry

The bon d'intervention must be **signed by the customer BEFORE the work starts** (research/26 section 8). Signed after, it is worthless, and the customer can legally cancel for 14 days after the work is done. Afrim is the only one on site. So "Afrim does no paperwork" and "the work order must be signed on site" pull against each other. This is a real open tension, not a detail.

**Claude's proposal, not a decided fact, for Roro to accept or refuse:**

- The van carries a stack of **pre-printed work orders**, both languages, already filled with everything that never changes (company block, the urgent-works waiver, the immediate-execution consent, the conditions acceptance, the 60 euro no-fix line, the guarantee exclusions).
- On site Afrim writes **two things only: the date and the price Roro gave him on the phone.**
- **The customer writes his own name and address and signs.** That is the part that legally matters, and it is the customer's hand, not Afrim's.
- Afrim ticks the surcharge box if there is one (evening, night, weekend), photographs the signed sheet with his phone, sends the photo to Roro, and leaves the paper copy with the customer. Roro keeps the photo.
- Total work for Afrim: two numbers, one tick, one photo. No sentences, no spelling, no maths.

**Question for Roro (one line, WhatsApp):** "Afrim only writes the date and the price on a paper the customer signs before we start, and sends you a photo. Is that OK for him?"

Until Roro answers, the last line of the WhatsApp template still asks whether a signed work order exists, so we always know which jobs are covered and which are not.

### Why the lines are in that order

They follow the Word invoice top to bottom: dates, then the FACTURÉ À block (name, address, VAT number if a company), then LIEU D'INTERVENTION, then the description lines, then the price, then the VAT table, then payment. Steph reads down the message and types down the page.

### The VAT lines (9 and 10), read this before changing them

Roro does NOT pick a VAT rate. He is never asked for one. He answers the two facts that decide it, and Steph applies the rate:

- **6 percent** only when all of this is true: the building is more than 10 years old, it is mainly a private home, and the bill goes to the person living there (research/26 section 3). Both answers yes.
- **21 percent** in every other case: newer building, business premises, landlord who does not live there, or a "je ne sais pas" that Roro cannot clear up. Any doubt goes to 21 percent, not to 6 percent, because the risk sits with us.
- **The long grey VAT sentence stays on the invoice, complete, always.** Never shortened, never edited.
- **Three services stay case by case** until the accountant answers in writing: septic emptying alone, camera inspection alone (the 149 euro one), and cellar pumping alone. When line 7 says one of those and nothing else, Steph does not invent a rate: she parks that invoice and asks. Inside a normal unblocking job on the same invoice, the camera line stays subordinate and follows the job's rate.
- **The word "nettoyage" / "reinigen" never appears on an invoice line.** High-pressure work is written "curage" or "débouchage" / "ontstoppen". The wrong word can turn 6 percent into 21 percent.

## 4. Private customer or business customer, the STOP test

Steph runs this test before she opens the Word file. It takes five seconds.

**Is the customer a Belgian company with its own VAT number that files periodic VAT returns?**

- **NO (a private person, a homeowner, a tenant):** the Word file is the right tool. PDF or paper, both legal. This is the bulk of the business.
- **YES (a company, a shop, a horeca, a landlord company):** **STOP. This one does NOT go out as a Word PDF.** Since 01.01.2026 domestic B2B invoices between Belgian VAT-registered businesses must go through **Peppol** as a structured e-invoice. A PDF by e-mail does not satisfy the duty (research/26 section 9). Steph already sends B2B through Peppol, so this side is compliant. Reverse charge applies on those: no rate, no VAT amount, and the exact autoliquidation sentence from research/26 section 4.
- **A syndic or association of co-owners (ACP / VME):** normally NOT a VAT taxable person, so it is **not** in the Peppol mandate and it is **not** reverse charge. It gets a normal invoice with VAT charged. Whether an ACP counts as "final consumer" for the 6 percent on common parts is not confirmed by an official text: it is on the accountant list, and until he answers, 21 percent is the safe answer.

So: **the Word file is the B2C instrument. Peppol is the B2B instrument.** They are not interchangeable, and one job never gets both.

### Where Peppol slots in later

Nothing changes while the volume is a few jobs a week. Steph's Word plus Peppol split is legal today and cheap. What would change it:

- **More invoices than one evening a week can absorb.** Word stops being free the moment it takes an hour a week; that is the real trigger, not a number of jobs.
- **Numbering by hand starts to slip.** Gaps, duplicates or a skipped month are the first hard sign that a tool is needed.
- **B2B becomes a real share of the work.** Then keeping the two instruments in sync by hand becomes the risk, and one tool that does B2C PDFs and B2B Peppol from the same numbering is worth paying for.
- **Before any move, three things must be true:** the numbering series carries over without a gap, the full 6 percent legal sentence prints verbatim (short forms circulating in tools do not count, research/26 section 3), and the 10 years of history stays exportable and readable. No tool is recommended here; that is a decision for Fady with real numbers on the table.

## 4b. The credit note, the rules (added 2026-09-08)

The credit note is its own document, French "note de crédit" and Dutch "creditnota", built and verified 2026-09-08 out of the same script as the invoices. **This file owns the RULES; the Word files themselves and their layout are owned by `assets/prepared/invoice/README.md`.**

- **Its own series, NC-2026-001, NC-2026-002, in order, no gaps** (research/26 section 10), separate from Steph's invoice numbering and never eating a number from it.
- **It names the invoice it corrects**, its number and its date, plus the reason. That reference is what makes a credit note a credit note.
- **The VAT sentence is verbatim from `research/26` section 10** when the corrected invoice bore VAT: FR "TVA à reverser à l'État dans la mesure où elle a été initialement déduite", NL "Btw terug te storten aan de Staat in de mate waarin ze oorspronkelijk in aftrek werd gebracht". Never shortened, never reworded.
- **A settlement block instead of a payment block.** Nothing is owed on a credit note, so the document says how the money goes back (refund or deduction from a later invoice) and carries the customer IBAN when it is refunded by transfer.
- **The amounts are written POSITIVE.** The document says on its face that the credit note takes them off the corrected invoice.
- **An issued invoice is never deleted and never overwritten.** A mistake on a sent invoice is corrected by a credit note, never by editing the invoice. A credit note against a B2B Peppol invoice goes out through Peppol, like the invoice it corrects (section 4).
